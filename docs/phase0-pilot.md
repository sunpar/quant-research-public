## Roles

One researcher holds every Phase 0 role: researcher owner, methodological reviewer, and integration owner. Review is therefore not independent. Cross-model reviews add a check but do not replace an independent reviewer. The same person writes the capability-evaluation tasks, knows their answers, and develops and steers the configurations they evaluate. A separate answer-key file cannot hide answers from their author, so these tasks are development cases, not held-out evaluations. A held-out comparison under [D011](decisions.md#d011-seal-capability-evaluation-answers-and-retire-disclosed-tasks) needs answer keys from someone outside development and steering.

The agent-authored supplied-candidate development baseline passed the P19 checks, per [D069](decisions.md#d069-record-a-reusable-development-baseline-through-the-research-loop). Its repeated synthetic outcomes do not change this ownership limit or establish the independent held-out Phase 4 comparison.

## Define the Phase 1 reference case

The known-answer signal is the real-time Sahm rule recession indicator. The pilot computes it from ALFRED vintages of the US unemployment rate, `UNRATE`, and checks it against FRED's published `SAHMREALTIME` series.

- **Decision time.** The decision for month m is made at the end of month m-1. It uses the latest `UNRATE` vintage whose ALFRED `realtime_start` is on or before that date. It recomputes the whole rolling calculation from that vintage's history, so seasonal revisions count. A fixed lag from the observation month can look ahead, because releases slip. BLS released the September 2025 data on 20 November 2025.
- **Indicator.** The indicator is the three-month average of `UNRATE` minus the lowest three-month average of the previous 12 months, as FRED defines `SAHMREALTIME`. The previous 12 months do not include the current month, so the indicator can be negative, as FRED's values are.
- **Allocation.** The decision for month m uses the latest indicator observation that the selected vintage can compute. That observation is normally for month m-2, and older when a release slips. Each decision records the observation month and the vintage's release date. The portfolio holds the market in month m when that indicator value is below 0.50. It holds one-month T-bills when the value is 0.50 or above. The first decision follows the same rule, and no other entry or exit condition exists.
- **Missing values.** October 2025 has no `UNRATE` value, because BLS did not collect that month's household survey. A window that contains a missing month averages, or takes the least of, its other months, and the missing month itself has no indicator value. This convention gives FRED's 0.43 for November 2025 and 0.35 for December 2025, and leaves October 2025 empty as FRED does. Carrying September forward gives 0.40 and 0.33 instead. [D053](decisions.md#d053-evaluate-signal-programs-per-decision-and-reconcile-the-sahm-series-with-fred) records the evidence. When a decision has no indicator value, the previous allocation holds.
- **Returns.** Returns are monthly, from the Ken French "Fama/French 3 Factors" file, with percentages converted to decimals. The portfolio return for month m is `RF + w × (Mkt-RF)`, where `w` is 1 in the market and 0 in T-bills. `Mkt-RF` alone is an excess return, not the return of a holding. These series are research returns, not tradable instruments.
- **Costs.** Each allocation switch costs 10 basis points of portfolio value, charged in the month the new allocation starts, including the sample's last month. The initial allocation and the end of the sample carry no cost. [D054](decisions.md#d054-backtest-allocations-with-a-plain-reference-and-a-vectorbt-engine) charges the cost on the portfolio value at the start of the switch month, so a switch month returns (1 - 1/1000) × (1 + RF + w × (Mkt-RF)) - 1.
- **Baseline.** The baseline is buy-and-hold market total return, `RF + (Mkt-RF)`.

## Accept the local pilot as a scoped Phase 1

Every [Phase 1](master-plan.md#phase-1-reproduce-a-trusted-experiment) exit test applies to the local pilot. Passing them accepts the pilot on public data and vectorbt. It does not accept the firm frameworks. The firm port is a later, separate acceptance. The firm adapters first rerun the same frozen public inputs and must agree with the local results. Only then does the pilot move to firm data.

- **Known answer.** The recomputed indicator equals `SAHMREALTIME` at FRED's two-decimal precision for every month in the reconciliation window. Means of two or three one-decimal rates lie on a grid of 1/60, so exact arithmetic with `Fraction` leaves no rounding ties against FRED's two decimals. The window starts at the first month whose whole input window has ALFRED vintages, which begin on 15 March 1960. It ends at the last month of the pinned `SAHMREALTIME` download. An unexplained mismatch blocks acceptance, and a month leaves the window only with a recorded reason. [D053](decisions.md#d053-evaluate-signal-programs-per-decision-and-reconcile-the-sahm-series-with-fred) records the live reconciliation and the months it excludes, and the researcher accepted those exclusions on 23 October 2026, per [D055](decisions.md#d055-run-the-reference-experiment-through-one-audited-pipeline-against-a-frozen-experiment-file).
- **Independent recomputation.** vectorbt and a plain Python calculation compute the strategy's monthly returns separately. Both must match hand-calculated cash flows for months that include a switch. A probe confirmed that vectorbt's fee and cash handling can express the rules above, and [D054](decisions.md#d054-backtest-allocations-with-a-plain-reference-and-a-vectorbt-engine) declares the tolerance between them, 1e-12 per month.
- **Flawed fixtures.** Two deliberately flawed variants must fail. One reads the current `UNRATE` vintage for every past decision. The other lags each observation month by a fixed two months.
- **Null and inconclusive results.** A controlled null fixture with an independently known answer must produce a null record. An inconclusive verdict on the real data is an accepted outcome.
- **Evaluation.** The [pilot evaluation profile](decisions.md#d015-evaluate-pilot-signals-against-their-recorded-trials) evaluates the strategy's returns against the baseline. Its subperiods are equal contiguous calendar parts, and their count is fixed before evaluation. The profile assumes independent, identically distributed errors, so its inference stays provisional until someone checks that assumption for monthly returns with rare switches. [reference.json](../src/quant_research/reference.json) freezes the window from April 1960 to August 2026, the cutoff, a minimum effect of 0.001 a month, 4 subperiods, alpha 0.05, and the buy-and-hold baseline, per [D055](decisions.md#d055-run-the-reference-experiment-through-one-audited-pipeline-against-a-frozen-experiment-file). An agent froze it under the user's autonomy grant on 22 October 2026, before any real return was evaluated, and the researcher accepted it on 23 October 2026, after the run.

The scoped Phase 1 ran on 23 October 2026 through `quant-research reference run`, and [D055](decisions.md#d055-run-the-reference-experiment-through-one-audited-pipeline-against-a-frozen-experiment-file) records the evidence. The reference package verified. Its reconciliation matched 780 of 800 months and excluded 20 with recorded reasons. vectorbt agreed with the plain engine within 3.5e-16 a month. The verdict was inconclusive, with an incremental return of 0.00115 a month and an interval from -0.00033 to 0.00263. Both flawed variants were rejected by the causality audit, and the controlled null fixture recorded null. The researcher accepted this scoped Phase 1 result on 23 October 2026. Phase 1 is complete for the local pilot on public data and vectorbt, and [Q003](open-questions.md#q003-what-independent-baseline-defines-agreement) is closed. The firm port remains a separate acceptance.

## Define the Phase 3 paper case

The paper case is Estrella and Mishkin (1998), "Predicting U.S. Recessions: Financial Variables as Leading Indicators", Review of Economics and Statistics 80(1), pages 45 to 61. The paper evaluates financial variables, the yield-curve spread among them, as predictors of recessions 1 to 8 quarters ahead.

The article is closed access. P21 registered the authors' Federal Reserve Bank of New York Research Paper 9609 of May 1996 instead, whose abstract matches the article's in substance, so the case reproduces that working paper ([D070](decisions.md#d070-register-the-estrella-and-mishkin-working-paper-and-its-fred-series)).

Before any result, choose one named table and model. Fix its sample, aggregation, forecast alignment, recession coding, and estimation in a [reproduction specification](decisions.md#d020-fix-the-reproduction-comparison-before-results). Classify the comparison as adapted data unless matched inputs are established. FRED's three-month bill series `TB3MS` is quoted on a discount basis, so check the paper's rate definition before using it.

P22 chose Table 2's SPREAD row and converted `TB3MS` to the paper's bond-equivalent basis. On adapted data it reproduced one of eight pseudo R² values within half a printed unit and left the eight t-statistics inconclusive ([D071](decisions.md#d071-reproduce-table-2s-spread-probit-on-adapted-fred-data)). The researcher approved the comparison basis and its choices on 23 October 2026 by accepting the coordinating agent's recommendation.

## Record exposure and confirmation

The researcher has seen the Sahm rule's historical performance, including its 2024 trigger, and knows the macro history of the sample. The models know that history too. All history is therefore development data. Confirmation is prospective only. The P16 procedure is registered under [D068](decisions.md#d068-register-campaign-procedures-before-later-released-outcomes), which separates historical initialization from original scored releases after registration. No real prospective observation is recorded yet. The signal switches rarely, so enough informative observations may take years.

A search holds the window from January 2005 back for its final evaluation, so its tools and experiments read no data available after 1 January 2005 ([D065](decisions.md#d065-run-experiments-inside-the-loop-under-a-frozen-search-method)). The researcher and the models know that window, so its final evaluation is development evidence too. The loop evaluates a frozen shortlist once, uses the campaign's declared trial budget for the adjustment, and obtains a separate conclusion and critique, per [D066](decisions.md#d066-interpret-held-back-results-with-a-separate-critique). Critic agreement cannot supply a missing supported verdict. Campaign packages now preserve the complete recorded run, its interpretation, and model cutoff qualifications, per [D067](decisions.md#d067-archive-each-research-ending-as-a-complete-campaign-snapshot). The package-only reproduction check adds no confirmation or capability evidence.

## Limit what reaches providers

Everything runs on this Mac: data, SurrealDB, the Temporal dev server, and backtests. The only network calls go to model providers and data sources.

The pilot calls models through the researcher's own Claude Code and Codex subscriptions, as D051 describes. Each subscription is its own provider route under [D041](decisions.md#d041-check-outbound-provider-content-against-the-run-and-a-versioned-provider-policy). The Claude Code route is `claude-subscription` ([D057](decisions.md#d057-call-claude-code-as-a-tool-disabled-completion-behind-a-local-first-turn-gate)). Each subscription call is charged as one model call with no cash cost, and tokens that the runtime does not fully report are marked unreported, per [D056](decisions.md#d056-make-the-workflow-the-single-accounting-owner-with-durable-call-receipts). Their policies admit public material only: the pilot's public data and papers, this repository's code, and development results. Restricted confirmation reports and firm data never reach them. A provider receives material only when the material's own approval names that provider, so firm material approved for the firm proxy cannot reach a personal subscription. Agent tool results carry the approvals of what they read ([D059](decisions.md#d059-render-each-tool-call-as-gateway-material-under-the-restrictions-of-what-it-read)). A campaign resolves into one run authority that reads only its permitted datasets. A dataset's contract takes that dataset's approvals, and only roles on a route whose live check recorded a go can activate ([D062](decisions.md#d062-resolve-a-campaign-revision-into-its-run-authority)). The Codex route is `codex-subscription`. It runs each call in a fresh app-server whose home holds only a link to the sign-in file, and it refuses to send when any tool, MCP server, or instruction file is reachable ([D058](decisions.md#d058-call-codex-through-a-fresh-app-server-in-an-isolated-home)). Each call keeps transcripts of what it sent and received, and its record names the runtime version and the model the subscription reports it served ([D061](decisions.md#d061-run-model-calls-and-tool-calls-as-activities-that-stop-their-adapters)). [D077](decisions.md#d077-align-recovery-records-and-pinned-lesson-authority) adds bounded failure reasons without storing provider error text in those reasons.
